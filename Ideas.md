Asteroids contain a wide variety of materials, but their composition varies significantly depending on the type of asteroid (e.g., C-type, S-type, M-type). Below is a generalized list of materials found in asteroids, sorted by estimated relative abundance (from most to least common). #### Metals
Iron (Fe) – Abundant in metallic asteroids (M-type) and as a component in silicates.
Nickel (Ni) – Commonly alloyed with iron in metallic asteroids.
Cobalt (Co) – Found in trace amounts in metallic asteroids.
Aluminum (Al) – Present in silicates and some metallic phases.

#### Water and Hydrated Minerals
Water Ice (H₂O) – Found in C-type and some D-type asteroids.
Hydroxyl (OH) – Bound in hydrated minerals like clays and serpentine.
Organic Compounds – Such as hydrocarbons, amino acids, and tar-like substances.

#### Notes on Abundance and Accessibility
C-type (Carbonaceous) asteroids are the most common and contain water, silicates, organic compounds, and hydrated minerals.
S-type (Stony) asteroids are rich in silicates and metals like iron and nickel.
M-type (Metallic) asteroids are composed primarily of metal alloys (iron-nickel) and contain platinum group metals and rare earth elements.
Volatile-rich asteroids (e.g., near-Earth objects) may contain water ice, ammonia, and methane, making them prime targets for fuel production.
Rare materials like gold, platinum, and rare earth elements are concentrated in metallic asteroids but are still present in much smaller quantities compared to iron, nickel, or silicates.
